# What counts as a learning

A learning is a sentence a future campaign can act on. It survives being read a year later by someone who was not there.

## Anatomy

Four parts, all required:

1. **The metric and its value.** "Trials per 1,000 impressions, 4.1 versus 3.1."
2. **What was compared.** The variants by name, not "variant A".
3. **The boundary.** What it does not prove. "On LinkedIn, to finance titles, over four weeks. Nothing about Meta or about other audiences."
4. **Confidence.** The verdict from code, with the sample and the interval. Never "clearly better".

A "why" may be added and must be labelled as a hypothesis. The what is evidence; the why is a story about the evidence, and next quarter someone will quote the story as fact unless it is marked.

## Refusals

Refuse to write a learning when:
- The verdict is `insufficient`. Say what sample would decide it instead.
- The comparison is confounded: different flight dates, a mid-flight edit, a bid change, a channel mix shift.
- The difference exists only in an aggregate that reverses within segments (Simpson's paradox). Report the segments.
- The result is a single campaign's channel-level total with no variant comparison behind it. That is a report line, not a learning.

A refusal with a reason is more valuable than a learning that will be contradicted. The graduation metric penalises learnings later contradicted more heavily than learnings not written.

## Scope

Learnings are client-level. "Proof-led beats pain-led" for this client, this audience, this product. It is not a fact about B2B marketing and must never be phrased as one, because it enters every future packet for this client and will shape copy for years.

## Confounds checklist

Days of week and length of flight equal? Budget shifted mid-flight? Creative edited after launch? Audience overlapping between sets? Seasonality or a client event in the window? Tracking changed? Any yes goes in the boundary.
