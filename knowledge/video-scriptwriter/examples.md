# Worked examples: Video Scriptwriter

## Example 1 — 15 seconds, sound off

| t | Visual | On screen | Audio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Screen capture: a spreadsheet with 4,100 rows, scrolling fast | "4,100 lines. Every month." | (ambient) |
| 3–7 | Cut: the product, an exceptions list with 23 items | "Or 23." | "Ledgerline matches the rest overnight." |
| 7–12 | Slow zoom on one exception, its rule visible | "You review what didn't match." | "You review what didn't match." |
| 12–15 | Text on brand colour, logo lower right | "Free 14-day trial. No card." | "Start a free trial." |

**Annotation.**
- The hook is a number and a contrast, visible by second three, with no brand yet.
- Read the on-screen column alone: 4,100 lines every month / or 23 / you review what didn't match / free trial. The story survives silence, which is how most people will see it.
- Everything is screen capture and a text card. A one-person team can make this in an afternoon.

## Example 2 — a product with nothing visual

**Problem.** A data product whose value is a number arriving on time. Nothing to film.

**Approach.** Text on brand colour as the primary medium, with typographic motion carrying the rhythm, and one real screen capture at the moment of proof. The script is written around the words because the words are the asset.

**Annotation.**
- The failure mode is stock footage of servers and abstract data visualisations, which says nothing and looks like every other data company.
- Admitting the product is not visual, and designing for that, produces a better film than pretending otherwise.

## Example 3 — a hook that fails and its fix

**Draft hook (0–3).** Logo animation, then "Ledgerline: reconciliation, reimagined."
**Problem.** Three seconds spent on brand and an adjective. Nothing has happened.

**Fixed.** A hand closing a laptop at 17:58 on the 1st of the month, with "The close is done. It's the 1st." Brand appears at second nine.

**Annotation.**
- The hook is a scene with an implicit claim. The viewer works out what it means, which is why they stay.
- Logo first is the most common brief a client gives and the most reliable way to lose the view.

Hook lands by 0:03; the on-screen column alone tells the story; one CTA; every claim approved; shot list producible by the client's actual team; safe zones noted per ratio.
