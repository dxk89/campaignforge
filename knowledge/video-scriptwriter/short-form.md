# Short-form scripts that work with the sound off

## Structure by duration

| Duration | Shape |
|---|---|
| 15s | Hook (0–3), one proof (3–10), ask (10–15). One idea only |
| 30s | Hook (0–3), problem (3–10), mechanism (10–22), ask (22–30) |
| 60s | Hook (0–3), problem (3–15), mechanism (15–40), proof (40–50), ask (50–60) |

The first three seconds decide whether the rest is watched. They must contain the idea, not the logo, not the brand, not a title card.

## Sound off first

Most feed views start muted. The on-screen text carries the message; the audio adds to it. Write the on-screen line for every beat and check that reading only those lines still tells the story. If it does not, the script depends on audio it will not get.

Keep on-screen text short per beat (roughly six to nine words), high contrast, and out of the platform's UI zones: bottom third on 9:16, and the corners where captions and buttons sit.

## Two columns and timecodes

Every script is visual, audio and on-screen text against a timecode. A paragraph of prose is not a script; it cannot be shot, timed or edited from.

## Shot lists a small team can actually make

Prefer, in order: screen capture of the real product, text on brand colour, simple b-roll of objects (a desk, a laptop closing, paper), a single talking head. Avoid anything needing a crew, a location, actors or a shoot day unless the brief says the client has them.

A script the client cannot produce is a script that does not exist.

## Rules

- One CTA, at the end, on screen and spoken.
- Every claim approved, same standard as any other asset.
- No logo before the hook has landed.
- Captions always, burned in or as an SRT.
- Aspect ratios: 9:16 for feed and stories, 1:1 for in-feed, 16:9 for the website and YouTube. Note the safe zones per ratio rather than reformatting blindly.
