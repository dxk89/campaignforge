# Paid social structure and testing

## Structure

Two campaigns minimum: prospecting and retargeting. They have different audiences, different creative and different economics, and merging them makes both unreadable.

**LinkedIn audiences** map to the MQL definition: job title or function plus seniority, company size, and industry where it discriminates. Skills and groups are weaker signals; member interests weaker still. Every audience should be traceable to a criterion in the MQL definition, so the leads it produces can actually qualify.

**Meta** works differently: broad targeting with strong creative usually beats narrow interest stacks, because the delivery system finds the audience faster than a marketer can define it. Use interests and lookalikes as secondary sets, not as the plan.

Audience size: LinkedIn sets below roughly 20,000–50,000 members deliver erratically and expensively. Ten sets of 2,000 is the classic mistake; it produces frequency, fatigue and no learning.

**Exclusions on every campaign:** existing customers, current opportunities, employees, and the retargeting pool from the prospecting sets. Missing exclusions means paying to advertise to people who already bought.

## Retargeting from lifecycle signals

The lifecycle already defines the signals that matter. Reuse them: visited pricing, clicked the proof email, started a trial without connecting. Each deserves different creative, because each person is stuck at a different point. Retargeting everyone who touched the site with one ad is a budget for reminding people that a company exists.

## Creative testing

One variable per test. The matrix is hook × proof × format; hold two constant and vary the third. A test that changes the headline, the image and the CTA teaches nothing about any of them.

Run tests where the sample can decide them (see `media-planner/allocation.md`). Name the variable in the plan, not just the variants.

## Fatigue

Watch frequency and CTR decay together. On LinkedIn, a tightly targeted set reaches fatigue quickly because the audience is small; a rising frequency with falling CTR is the signal, and the fix is new creative, not a bid change. Plan the refresh into the flight: new hooks at the two-week mark, not when performance has already collapsed.

Benchmarks for what "falling" means are in `shared/b2b-benchmarks.md`: single-image LinkedIn sponsored content sits around 0.44–0.65% CTR, and below roughly 0.35% is a creative or targeting problem rather than bad luck.
