# Worked examples: Paid Social Specialist

## Example 1 — audiences traceable to the MQL

**MQL.** Finance title; 50–500 employees; trial created; one engagement signal.

**LinkedIn prospecting sets.**
1. Job function Finance + seniority Manager and above + company size 51–500 + region. Roughly 90,000 members. Ads: variants 1 and 2.
2. Job titles (Controller, Financial Controller, Head of Finance, Finance Director) + same size and region. Roughly 35,000. Ads: variant 1 and a title-specific hook.

**Exclusions on both.** Customer list, open opportunities, employees, website visitors in the last 30 days.

**Annotation.**
- Both sets map to MQL criteria 1 and 2 exactly, so their leads can qualify. An audience built on "interested in accounting software" would produce leads that fail fit scoring and sales would blame the campaign.
- Two sets, not ten. The second is narrower and exists to test whether title-level targeting justifies its higher CPM; that is a real question with a decidable answer.

## Example 2 — a test that isolates one variable

**Question.** Does leading with the number beat leading with the pain?

**Setup.** Same audience, same image, same CTA. Variant 1 opens "Your finance team loses about a week a month…"; variant 2 opens "Ledgerline customers close the month four days faster." Everything after the first sentence is identical.

**Metric.** Trials per 1,000 impressions. Decision rule: after 500 clicks per variant, shift 70% of budget to the winner if the gap exceeds 20%.

**Annotation.**
- Only the opening changes, so the result means something about openings.
- A junior tests "creative A vs creative B" where both differ in headline, image and offer, and then cannot say what to do next.
- Trials per 1,000 impressions rather than CTR: CTR measures curiosity, and the campaign is not buying curiosity.

## Example 3 — retargeting by where they stopped

| Signal | Audience | Creative |
|---|---|---|
| Visited pricing, no trial | Pricing viewers, 30 days | The offer: 14 days, no card |
| Trial created, nothing connected | From CRM sync | The mechanism: what connecting one processor shows you |
| Clicked email 2, no trial | From the lifecycle | The proof, extended: the walkthrough |

**Annotation.**
- Three audiences, three different reasons for stopping, three different messages. One retargeting ad for all three would speak to none of them.
- These signals already exist because the Ops Architect defined them. The paid plan reuses the lifecycle rather than inventing a parallel set of triggers, which is what keeps the reporting coherent.

## Example 4 — a tiny addressable audience

**Situation.** The exact ICP is about 6,000 members on LinkedIn.

**Plan.** One set at the full 6,000 with a low frequency cap, plus a broader set at function level with creative that self-selects (a first line that names the job precisely). Stated in the plan: at this size, expect fatigue within three weeks and budget a creative refresh at day 14; the broad set is there to keep learning when the narrow one saturates.

**Annotation.**
- Naming the saturation date in advance is the difference between a plan and a hope. A junior runs the 6,000-member set flat for four weeks and reports "declining performance" in week three as if it were a surprise.

---

## Self-check applied
Every audience maps to an MQL criterion; one variable per test with the variable named; retargeting uses lifecycle signals; exclusions on every campaign; refresh planned into the flight.
