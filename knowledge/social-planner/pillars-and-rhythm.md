# Pillars, rhythm and the hook

A month of B2B social is a rhythm, not thirty-two adverts. The campaign angle runs through it; most posts are useful or interesting on their own terms.

## Pillars and shares

| Pillar | Share | Job | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| educate | 35% | Something the reader can use today | It teaches what the reader already knows |
| proof | 20% | A specific result, quote or number from approved claims | There is no approved claim and it gets invented |
| product | 15% | What it does, plainly | It becomes a feature list |
| point-of-view | 20% | An opinion the company holds, from the strategy angles | It is a platitude, or it insults a named competitor |
| engage | 10% | A question that invites a reply | It is "thoughts?" under a link |

If the context has no proof points, reallocate proof to educate and say so. Never fill a proof slot with a claim that has no source.

## The series device

One recurring format a week, same day, recognisable at a glance: a number, a checklist, a myth corrected, a reader question. Recognition is what turns thirty-two posts into a programme. Name it in `pillars`.

## Channel fit

- **LinkedIn** — educate and point-of-view. The first line before "see more" is the post for most impressions; write it as a complete thought. Native, first-person writing outperforms brand-voice writing by a wide margin on this platform.
- **X** — engage and short educate. A complete thought under 280 including hashtags. Not a thread.
- **Instagram** — proof and product, always with a graphic. The caption serves the image, not the reverse.

## Rhythm rules

1. One idea per week per pillar. The same point on Monday and Thursday reads as an empty calendar.
2. Build to a launch, then follow through: two posts before the date, one on it, one after with a result or a reaction.
3. Blackout dates are absolute. Holidays take educate and engage, not product.
4. Vary the opening move: a number, a scene, a correction, a question, a quote. Five posts opening "Most finance teams..." is one voice with nothing to say.
5. Write every hook first, across the whole month, then the bodies. Weak hooks are visible in a list and invisible one post at a time.

## Hook patterns that hold

Number ("Twenty-three exceptions out of 4,100 lines"), scene ("It is the 6th and the close is not done"), correction ("Reconciliation is not a bank rec problem"), cost ("A week a month, every month"), question that only the audience can answer ("What day do you actually close?"), verbatim ("'We close on the 2nd now'").

Not: "In today's fast-paced world", "We are excited to announce", the company name, or a rhetorical question the reader would answer "no" to.
