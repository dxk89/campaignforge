# The page as an argument

A landing page is one argument in five moves, in this order. Every section earns the next scroll or the page ends there.

1. **Promise (hero).** Message match with the ad that sent the click: the hero echoes the hook, in the same words where possible. A visitor who cannot tell within two seconds that they are in the right place leaves, and that mismatch is the single largest source of wasted spend on a campaign.
2. **Proof.** Approved claims only, each carrying its claim id. Three is usually enough; a wall of logos with no numbers proves nothing.
3. **Mechanism.** How it works, plainly, in three or four steps. This is what converts the sceptical: they need to believe it is possible, not just desirable.
4. **Objections.** The two or three anxieties from the audience research, answered in the client's voice. Not an FAQ dump; the specific fears that stop this buyer.
5. **Ask.** One call to action, repeated, phrased as what the visitor gets.

## The form

Derived from the MQL definition, never from what would be nice to know. For each MQL criterion, either a field or a documented inference (company size from the email domain, sector from enrichment). Every field costs conversion, so a field that qualifies nobody is pure loss.

Rules: six fields maximum; work email rather than phone at first contact in B2B; no field whose answer the visitor would have to look up; a consent line that states the purpose, not a checkbox with a link to a policy nobody reads.

## Message match in practice

If the ad said "Close the month four days faster", the hero says "Close the month four days faster", not "Streamline your financial operations". Rewriting the promise between the ad and the page is the most common self-inflicted conversion wound in B2B.

## One CTA

Repeated at the hero, after the proof and at the end, always the same words. A secondary CTA ("or book a demo") splits the decision and reduces both. If the campaign genuinely needs two paths, that is two pages.

## Accessibility

Every form field labelled, contrast passing, headings in order, a visible focus state. Run `check_accessibility`; serious and critical violations block. A page a screen reader cannot complete is a page that loses leads and, in the EU, a compliance exposure.
