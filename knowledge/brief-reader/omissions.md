# What briefs leave out

Extract only what is stated. The value beyond the five fields is the `notes` field: mandatories, prohibitions and the twelve things briefs usually omit.

## Copy these verbatim

Every sentence containing "must", "always", "never", "do not", "avoid", "only", or a sign-off name. Quote them; do not summarise. "Do not call the data real-time" and "avoid over-claiming on freshness" are different instructions, and a summary of the first produces the second.

## The twelve omissions

1. Success metric and target
2. Budget
3. Landing page or destination
4. Legal or regulatory constraints
5. What was run before, and how it did
6. Rules about naming competitors
7. Tone by example rather than by adjective
8. Audience exclusions (existing customers, competitors, regions)
9. Timing: live dates, blackouts, events
10. Who approves what
11. Where proof points come from
12. What must not be said

Report which are missing. A brief with an objective and no metric is not a bad brief; it is a brief with a question the person may not have noticed.

## Never infer

Tone from the document's own writing style. An objective from the product type. A language from the client's location. A budget from the ambition. Each of these feels helpful and each puts a guess where the person expects their own words.
