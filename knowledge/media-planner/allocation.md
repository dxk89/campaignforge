# Allocating a budget

## Channel by objective

| Objective | Weight toward | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| brand_awareness | Broad reach: LinkedIn broad targeting, trade media, video, podcasts | Most of the audience is out-market; unique reach beats frequency |
| lead_generation, trial_signups | Intent: search first, then LinkedIn with tight targeting | Buying existing demand converts at the lowest cost per lead |
| event_registrations | LinkedIn plus owned email, with a short flight | A date needs frequency close to it, and the list is the cheapest audience |

Search captures demand that already exists; it does not create it. LinkedIn creates and captures at a higher unit cost but with better fit. Meta is cheaper per lead and needs more nurturing before those leads are sales-ready. Choose by what the campaign has to do, then check the audience is actually reachable there.

## Minimum viable budget

A channel that cannot produce enough clicks to decide its experiment should not be in the plan. Work backwards: at the channel's benchmark conversion rate, how many clicks are needed to detect the difference the experiment is looking for? At its benchmark CPC, what does that cost? If the allocation is below that, cut the channel and give its budget to one that clears the bar.

Three channels on a small budget usually means three inconclusive tests. Two channels with enough spend to decide beats three that teach nothing.

## Flighting

Front-load learning: weeks one and two spread across variants to find what works, weeks three and four concentrated on winners. Reserve roughly 20% for the second half so there is budget to scale into. A flat daily spend across four weeks learns slowly and scales nothing.

For events, weight the last ten days before the date and keep a small presence earlier so the name is not new.

## Forecasting

Always a range, never a point, always with the benchmark date. Use the ranges in `shared/b2b-benchmarks.md` and say which row was used. State the assumption chain: spend ÷ CPC = clicks; clicks × conversion = leads; leads × MQL rate = MQLs. Any of the three can be wrong and the client should see where.

Where the client has their own history, use it instead and say so. A client's own numbers from last quarter beat any published benchmark.
