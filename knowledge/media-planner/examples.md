# Worked examples: Media Planner

## Example 1 — a budget too small for three channels

**Brief.** EUR 12,000, four weeks, trial signups, finance leads at SaaS companies.

**Naive plan.** EUR 4,000 each to Search, LinkedIn and Meta.

**What the maths says.** At LinkedIn's B2B range (CPC roughly $5–15, call it EUR 8), EUR 4,000 buys about 500 clicks. To decide a two-variant test at a ~5% landing conversion rate needs several hundred clicks *per variant*. Four thousand euros on LinkedIn produces one inconclusive test, not two conclusive ones.

**Plan submitted.** Search EUR 4,500 (36%), LinkedIn EUR 6,500 (54%), Meta EUR 1,000 (8%) as a reach test only with no experiment attached, EUR 0 held. Rationale states: Meta is included at a level that buys reach data and explicitly cannot decide a creative test; if the client wants a Meta test, it needs EUR 3,000 more or one of the other channels drops.

**Expected volumes.** Search: EUR 4,500 ÷ $5.42 CPC ≈ 750–900 clicks; at 8% conversion, 60–72 trials. LinkedIn: EUR 6,500 at EUR 5–12 CPC ≈ 540–1,300 clicks; at 3–6% landing conversion, 16–78 trials. Ranges, with the benchmark date, and the assumption chain shown.

**Annotation.**
- The LinkedIn range is wide because the CPC range is wide. Presenting a single number here would be false precision on the largest line of the budget.
- Naming what Meta's allocation cannot do is the most useful sentence in the plan.

## Example 2 — the audience is not where the plan assumed

**Brief.** Brand awareness for a product sold to port operations managers.

**Audience research.** Almost no presence on LinkedIn beyond job titles; the audience reads two trade publications and attends one annual conference.

**Plan.** LinkedIn reduced to a title-targeted retargeting layer only; the majority to trade media placement and the conference; a small search allocation on the specific problem terms found in the research.

**Annotation.**
- The channel shortlist comes from where the audience is, not from where the tools are convenient. A LinkedIn-heavy plan for this audience would have spent the budget reaching people who match a title filter and never see the ad.
- Trade media has no published benchmark in our pack. The plan says so and asks for the publisher's own rate card and audited circulation rather than forecasting from nothing.

## Example 3 — flighting an event

**Brief.** Event registrations, event on day 28, EUR 8,000.

**Flight.** Week 1: 15% (awareness that the event exists, broad). Week 2: 20%. Week 3: 30%. Week 4: 35%, weighted to the last five days, with the final two days on retargeting only.

**Annotation.**
- Registrations cluster near the date; a flat flight spends most of its money when nobody is ready to commit.
- The early 15% is not wasted: it makes the last week's ads a reminder rather than an introduction.
- Retargeting-only at the end is the cheapest registrations in the campaign.

## Self-check applied
Every channel can decide its experiment or says it cannot; shares sum to 100; volumes are ranges with the benchmark date and the assumption chain; channels without audience presence excluded and the exclusion explained.
