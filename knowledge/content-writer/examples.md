# Worked examples: Content Writer

## Example 1 — conclusion first

**Junior opening.** "Month-end close is a challenge for finance teams everywhere. In this article we will explore the causes of reconciliation delays and consider some possible approaches."

**Rewritten.** "The last week of your month is not a reconciliation problem. It is a matching problem, and matching is the part software does better than people. Here is where the days actually go, and what changes when the matching is done before you open the file."

**Annotation.**
- The claim, the mechanism and the promise, in three sentences. A reader who stops here still knows what the piece thinks.
- "We will explore" is a writer describing their own plan. Nobody reads for the plan.

## Example 2 — an arguable claim with the counter-argument handled

**Claim.** "Most finance teams should not automate their close. They should automate their matching and leave the close alone."

**Counter-argument, in the piece.** "The case against this is real: piecemeal automation leaves you with two systems and a seam between them, and seams are where audits go wrong. If your close is genuinely simple and your volume is low, one system that does everything adequately beats two that each do one thing well."

**Annotation.**
- The counter-argument is stated at its strongest and given a condition under which it wins. That is what makes the main claim credible rather than promotional.
- A junior either omits it or presents a weak version to knock down, which readers notice.

## Example 3 — a case study with its complication

**Structure.**
- *Situation.* A 180-person SaaS company, two processors, close on the 8th.
- *Complication.* They tried automating inside their accounting system first. It matched bank lines but not processor payouts net of fees, so the exceptions it produced were the same lines they had been matching by hand, plus a new report to check.
- *Resolution.* Matching moved in front of the ledger; the accounting system receives matched entries.
- *Result.* [approved claim, with its source]

**Annotation.**
- The complication is the whole value. It tells a reader in the same position why the obvious first attempt fails, which is the thing they cannot find anywhere else.
- The result is a placeholder until an approved claim exists. Writing a plausible number and marking it "example" is how invented figures end up in published case studies.

---

## Self-check applied
Conclusion in the first paragraph; one arguable claim, argued; counter-argument at its strongest and answered; every number an approved claim; no throat-clearing; subheadings that say something.
