# Channel craft

What each channel truncates, what the format rewards, and the rule that follows. Limits themselves live in `shared/platform-specs.md` and generate `limits.js`; this pack is about craft.

## Meta

- The feed truncates primary text after roughly two lines on mobile. The hook is the first sentence or there is no hook.
- The headline is read second, at a glance, often without the primary text. It must stand alone.
- Description shows inconsistently by placement; treat it as a bonus, never as where the offer lives.
- Rule: hook, then proof, then ask. One idea per variant.

## LinkedIn

- The first line before "see more" is the whole ad for most impressions. Write it as a complete thought.
- Single-image sponsored content sits around 0.44–0.65% CTR; below 0.35% is a creative or targeting problem, not bad luck.
- Thought-leader format performs several times better on CTR and cost per click than single image, because it reads as a person rather than a brand. When the client has a credible individual, recommend it and write in first person.
- Document ads earn strong click-through for mid-funnel offers and usually lower cost per lead than a generic lead form.
- Rule: native voice, first person where possible, a specific number early, no brand throat-clearing.

## Google responsive search ads

- Headlines are combinatorial: any three may show in any order. Each must be a complete, sensible line on its own.
- The product name belongs in at least two headlines; a term from the ad group's keywords in at least three. Relevance to the group is what makes the ad specific.
- Pin sparingly; pinning everything defeats the format.
- Descriptions carry the proof and the offer, not a second headline.
- Rule: write the headlines first. They force specificity that the rest of the campaign then inherits.

## Email

- One job per email: introduce, prove, ask or handle the objection.
- One call to action. Single-CTA emails outperform multi-link emails on click rate by a wide margin.
- Subject lines of roughly six to ten words fit the mobile preview; longer is cut.
- Preview text is a second subject line, not a repeat of the first.
- Do not forecast from open rates: Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates reported opens by roughly 8–18%. Click rate is the metric that means something.
- Branch on behaviour, not on time alone: a click on the proof email is the signal that separates the interested from the enrolled.
- Rule: write the body first, the subject last, and cut the body by a fifth before the subject is written.

## Cross-channel

- The same lead angle everywhere; variants differ by hook and proof, never by angle.
- A number with a date and a population beats an adjective every time.
- If a line would survive on a competitor's site with the brand swapped, it is not differentiated. Rewrite it.

Sources: platform documentation for Meta, LinkedIn and Google Ads; LinkedIn and email benchmark compilations 2026 (see `shared/b2b-benchmarks.md` for figures and their sources). Craft rules are this repository's own, derived from those specs and benchmarks.
