# Awareness stages and category entry points

Two frames the Strategist uses on every brief. The first decides what the angle must do; the second decides what it must attach itself to.

## Stages of awareness (Schwartz)

Where the reader is decides the job of the first line.

| Stage | The reader | The angle's job | Lead with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Does not know they have the problem | Name the problem in their language | A situation they recognise |
| Problem-aware | Feels the pain, does not know solutions exist | Show that this is solvable | The pain, then the mechanism |
| Solution-aware | Knows the category, not the brands | Differentiate | Contrast, mechanism, proof |
| Product-aware | Knows you, has not decided | Remove the doubt | Proof, objection handling, offer |
| Most-aware | Ready | Make it easy | The offer and the CTA |

Three angles are genuinely different when they sit at different stages or attach to different entry points. Three phrasings of the same stage is one angle.

## The 95:5 rule and category entry points

Research from the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute with LinkedIn's B2B Institute finds that <cite index="80-1">only about 5% of potential B2B buyers are in the market at any moment; the other 95% are out-market and not researching solutions, even though they may later</cite>. <cite index="79-1">B2B purchase cycles are long: most companies buy computers about once every four years and change banking services about once every five</cite>.

Two consequences for strategy:

1. **Most of the audience cannot act now.** <cite index="79-2">The job for them is to link the brand to the buying situations that will bring them into the category, so the brand is what comes to mind when they do enter</cite>. Those situations are category entry points: "our system can no longer keep up with our growth", "our current software is out of support", "we have added a second payment processor".
2. **Judging brand work by immediate leads is the error.** <cite index="82-1">If 95% of the audience cannot buy immediately, most of the effect is delayed</cite>. Awareness campaigns are measured on reach, share of voice, branded search and category association; activation campaigns on leads and pipeline.

The Binet and Field balance follows from this: long-term growth needs both brand building and activation, with B2B often near an even split. Objective decides the emphasis:

- brand_awareness → emotional, distinctive, attached to entry points, broad reach
- lead_generation, trial_signups → rational, specific, attached to the in-market 5%, intent channels
- event_registrations → a date and a reason, close to activation but with a reach element

## How the Strategist uses this

1. Place the audience on the awareness scale using the research, not the brief's assumption.
2. List the category entry points the company context supports (situations, not features).
3. Build one angle per stage or entry point that the proof supports.
4. Choose the lead by objective: activation objectives take the in-market angle, awareness objectives take the entry-point angle.
5. Say in `lead_reasoning` which entry point the lead attaches to and what is given up.

Figures are as published by those institutes.
