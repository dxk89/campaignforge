# Account structure and intent

## Classify every term before grouping

| Intent | Looks like | Buy it? |
|---|---|---|
| Transactional | "reconciliation software pricing", "buy X" | Yes, first |
| Commercial | "best payout reconciliation tool", "X vs Y" | Yes, with comparison copy |
| Informational | "how to reconcile stripe payouts" | Only with a genuine content offer; expect low conversion |
| Navigational | "ledgerline login", competitor brands | Own brand yes; competitor brands only with legal sign-off |

One intent per ad group. Mixing "how to reconcile" and "reconciliation software" in one group produces an ad that serves neither and a landing page that matches neither.

## Groups

Theme by intent and by feature, tightly enough that every keyword in the group could sensibly be answered by the same three headlines. Ten keywords is a healthy group; a hundred is a bucket. Each group points at one landing page whose copy contains the group's terms.

## Match types

Phrase and exact for control. Broad only where conversion volume is strong enough to feed automation, and never in a new account with no conversion history. Every group needs its own negatives; the account needs a shared list.

## Negatives, day one

Job seekers ("jobs", "salary", "career", "internship"), free ("free", "open source", "template", "excel"), education ("what is", "course", "tutorial" unless the group is informational by design), competitor brands unless deliberately targeted, and the obvious wrong-industry homonyms for the client's terms.

Missing negatives is the fastest way to spend a budget on nothing. Build the list before the campaign runs, not after the first report.

## RSA craft

Headlines are combinatorial: any three may show in any order, so each must be a complete line that makes sense on its own. Rules:
- The product name in at least two.
- A term from the group's keywords in at least three, phrased naturally.
- The offer in exactly one.
- Pin sparingly. Pinning everything turns the format back into an expanded text ad and forfeits its advantage.
- Descriptions carry proof and offer, not a second headline.

## Policy

No trademarked competitor terms in ad text. Comparative claims must be substantiated to the same standard as any other claim (see `shared/uk-advertising-claims.md`). Superlatives need proof or they do not run.
