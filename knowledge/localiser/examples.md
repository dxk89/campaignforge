# Worked examples: Localiser

---

## Example 1 — a headline that only fits in English

**English (RSA, 30 max).** "Close the Month 4 Days Faster" (29)

**Literal.** "Feche o Mês 4 Dias Mais Rapidamente" (35). Over.

**Adapted.** "Feche o Mês 4 Dias Mais Cedo" (28).

**Annotation.**
- "Mais cedo" replaces "mais rapidamente" and is both shorter and more natural for a deadline: the close happens *earlier*, not *faster*. The better Portuguese is also the shorter one, which is usually the case when the idea is reconsidered rather than the words trimmed.
- A junior submits the 35-character version and lets the platform truncate, or writes "Feche o Mês Mais Rápido" and loses the number, which was the whole claim.

## Example 2 — register, not translation

**English LinkedIn intro.** "Your finance team loses about a week a month to payout matching. Ledgerline customers get four of those days back."

**Adapted.** "A sua equipa financeira perde cerca de uma semana por mês a conciliar pagamentos. Os clientes da Ledgerline recuperam quatro desses dias."

**Annotation.**
- "A sua equipa" addresses the company, which is the Portuguese business default; "você" would have been the Brazilian-sounding choice and "a tua equipa" would be wrong in a professional context.
- "a conciliar" is the European progressive. "conciliando" would be the Brazilian gerund and is the fastest way to mark copy as foreign.
- The claim keeps its number and its subject ("os clientes da Ledgerline"), because attribution matters more in a market where the brand is unknown.
- Length: 137 against 121 in English, within the soft target. Where it would not have been, the second sentence loses "desses dias" and gains "quatro dias".

## Example 3 — a pun that cannot survive

**English email subject.** "Where the last week of your month goes"

This is not a pun, and it transfers: "Para onde vai a última semana do seu mês" (40 of 60). Kept.

**A real pun, from a different campaign.** "Balancing act" as a headline for a reconciliation product, playing on balance sheets.

**Adapted.** Not translated. "Ato de equilíbrio" carries the circus image and none of the accounting one. Replaced with "As contas certas, no dia certo", which does the same job (a promise of accuracy and timeliness) with a Portuguese construction that sounds written rather than converted.

**Annotation.**
- The rule: replace the device, keep the job. Ask what the English line was doing, not what it said.
- Record the substitution in the glossary if the line recurs across a campaign, so every asset uses the same replacement.

## Example 4 — the numeric trap

**English.** "Connects to 2,400 banks."
**Wrong.** "Liga-se a 2,400 bancos." — reads as two point four in Portugal.
**Right.** "Liga-se a 2.400 bancos."

**Annotation.**
- A decimal comma error in a finance product's ad is the worst possible place for it. The compliance scanner does not catch this; the self-check must.
- Same family: "1 bilhão" in Brazilian is 10⁹; in European Portuguese that is "mil milhões" and "bilião" is 10¹². Any large number needs checking, not translating.
