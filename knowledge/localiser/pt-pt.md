# European Portuguese for B2B campaigns

Adaptation, not translation. The intent, the angle, the proof and the call to action survive; the words are rewritten for a Portuguese business reader.

## Register

Portuguese B2B copy sits a step more formal than its English equivalent. Address the reader with the implied polite third person ("Saiba como a sua equipa…", "Peça uma demonstração"), or address the company rather than the person ("a sua empresa"). Never "tu". Never an explicit "você", which reads as Brazilian in Portuguese marketing copy even where it is grammatically available.

Imperatives are normal in CTAs and are not rude: "Comece o teste", "Fale connosco", "Veja como funciona".

## Length

Portuguese runs roughly 15–25% longer than English. A 30-character Google headline does not survive a literal translation. Tactics, in order of preference:

1. Drop the article or possessive where Portuguese allows it.
2. Replace a phrase with a single verb ("faça a reconciliação" → "concilie").
3. Cut the qualifier, keep the noun.
4. Change the idea to a shorter true one with the same intent.
5. Never truncate. A cut sentence reads as a mistake, not as brevity.

## Idiom and culture

- English puns, sports metaphors and alliteration do not transfer. Replace the device, keep the job the line was doing.
- Numbers: decimal comma, thousands separated by a space or a full stop, currency after the figure ("190 €" or "EUR 190"), dates as dd/mm/yyyy.
- Formality of address in email: "Bom dia," or "Caro(a) [name]," rather than a bare first name for a first contact.
- Portuguese advertising is governed by the Código da Publicidade with self-regulation through Auto Regulação Publicitária; the substantiation principles match the English ones in `shared/uk-advertising-claims.md`. A claim that fails there fails here.

## Glossary

Product names, feature names and brand terms come from the research pass glossary and are used exactly as it says. When in doubt, leave a product name untranslated: a translated product name is unfindable in search and unrecognisable in a demo.
