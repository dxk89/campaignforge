# Finding the buyer in their own words

Two methods. The first says what to look for; the second says how to capture it.

## The four forces of progress

(cite index="86-1">Bob Moesta and Chris Spiek developed the four-forces model as part of the Jobs-to-be-Done interviewing method</cite>. It maps a single customer's decision to switch:

| Force | Direction | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Push** of the current situation | toward change | "We missed the close deadline again" |
| **Pull** of the new solution | toward change | "I saw a demo where the matching was already done" |
| **Anxiety** about the new | against change | "What if the numbers don't tie and I can't explain why" |
| **Habit** of the present | against change | "The spreadsheet works. I built it. I know it" |

(cite index="86-2">A switch happens only when push and pull outweigh anxiety and habit; flip the inequality and the customer stays put however good the product is</cite>. (cite index="86-3">Most teams work on making the product more attractive and ignore the two forces that kill most deals</cite>.

Consequences for research:

- Collect all four. An audience file that is all pains is half a picture and produces copy that pushes without ever addressing what holds people back.
- Anxiety and habit are rarely stated outright. They appear as hedges, as questions about migration and audit trails, as "we looked at it last year and did nothing".
- (cite index="90-1">Non-consumption, the people who want progress and do not act, is the largest and most invisible market</cite>. In B2B that is usually the status quo: a spreadsheet, a manual process, a person.

The campaign uses them directly: push and pull become the hook and the proof; anxiety becomes email 3 and the landing page objections; habit becomes the switching-cost story and the trial's low friction.

## Voice-of-customer mining

Rules for turning sources into usable language:

1. **Verbatim or nothing.** "Spend Friday afternoon reconciling Stripe" is usable; "reconciliation challenges" is not. Smoothing a quote into marketing language destroys the only thing that made it valuable.
2. **Cite everything.** Every phrase, pain and objection carries the URL it came from. An uncited phrase is dropped before submit.
3. **Source hierarchy.** Practitioner communities, reviews and support forums first; analyst and trade coverage second; vendor content last and only for competitor messages.
4. **Search like a practitioner, not a marketer.** Add "reddit", "vs", "alternative", "anyone else", "how do you". Marketing phrases return marketing pages.
5. **Tag by force, then rank** by how often it recurs and how much energy is in it. Frequency alone promotes the dull.
6. **Declare thin results.** An audience that barely talks online is a finding. Padding it with plausible pains is the failure mode that ends in copy addressed to nobody.

## Search terms

The phrases people type are the phrases they think in. Use `autocomplete` on the seed terms and record what comes back; those go to the Search Specialist as ad group seeds and to the Copywriter as the reader's own words. Mark them with their source so the writer knows they are typed, not written.
