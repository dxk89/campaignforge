# Worked examples: Customer Researcher

The verbatims below are written for this example. In production every one carries a URL, and any that cannot is dropped.

---

## Example 1 — all four forces, not just pains

**Audience.** Finance leads at 50–500 person SaaS companies.

**Output (abridged).**

```
push:    "We closed on the 8th last month. The board asks why and I say 'Stripe'."
         "One person owns the recon sheet and she was on holiday in August."
pull:    "I saw a walkthrough where the matching was already done when they opened it."
anxiety: "If the numbers don't tie, I have to explain the tool to the auditor as well as the numbers."
         "Another integration to maintain."
habit:   "The spreadsheet works. I built it. I know where everything is."
```

**Annotation.**
- A junior returns six pains and no anxiety or habit, and the campaign then pushes harder at people who were never going to move for lack of push. (cite index="86-2">The switch needs push and pull to outweigh anxiety and habit</cite>; copy that ignores half the equation argues with itself.
- The habit quote is the most useful line in the file. It tells the Copywriter that the enemy is not a competitor and the Ops Architect that the trial must produce a visible result before the habit reasserts.
- The anxiety about the auditor becomes email 3 and the landing page objection block. The anxiety about maintenance becomes the connector proof.
- Each is tagged with its force and carries its URL. Untagged verbatims are just quotes.

## Example 2 — a thin audience, declared

**Audience.** Compliance officers at Portuguese insurance brokers.

**Output.**

```
who: "Thin results. Four relevant threads and two reviews across eight searches. This audience is
      largely absent from open forums; what follows is drawn from those six sources and should be
      treated as indicative, not representative. A short round of client interviews would be worth
      more than more searching."
language: [three phrases, all cited]
pains:    [two, cited]
sources:  [six URLs]
```

**Annotation.**
- Six sources, six citations, and a plain statement of the limit. The recommendation to interview is the honest next step and costs the client nothing to hear.
- A junior fills all the lists to look complete, and every downstream agent then treats invention as evidence. This is the single most damaging failure this agent can make, because nothing downstream can detect it.
- Short lists with citations beat long lists without. The rubric rewards the former.

## Example 3 — searching past vendor content

**Problem.** "Payout reconciliation" returns vendor pages for three results deep.

**Searches that worked.**
- `reconcile stripe payouts reddit` — practitioner threads with the language
- `"month end close" spreadsheet anyone else` — the phrasing people use when venting
- `xero stripe reconciliation not matching` — the specific failure, where the real complaints live
- `search_hn "reconciliation"` — technical finance discussions with permalinks

**Searches that failed.**
- `best reconciliation software` — a listicle farm
- `finance team pain points` — marketing content about marketing content
- `reconciliation challenges 2026` — vendor blogs quoting each other

**Annotation.**
- The pattern: search the failure, not the category. People write about what broke, not about "challenges".
- Adding a platform name ("reddit") or a community's phrasing ("anyone else") is what reaches practitioners.
- `autocomplete` on "reconcile stripe" gave the terms the Search Specialist later used as ad group seeds, and those are marked with their source so nobody mistakes them for written copy.
