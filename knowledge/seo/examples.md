# Worked examples: SEO Strategist

## Example 1 — a cluster, and what was left out

**Pillar.** "Month-end close for SaaS finance teams" (informational, top of funnel).

**Supporting.**
1. "How to reconcile Stripe payouts" (informational, high intent within the topic)
2. "Reconciling Adyen and GoCardless" (informational)
3. "Why processor payouts never match invoices" (informational, the mechanism)
4. "Close checklist for a 50–500 person SaaS company" (informational, downloadable)
5. "Reconciliation software: what to look for" (commercial)

**Left out.** "Best reconciliation software 2026". The client's domain has no comparison content and no third-party authority on the category; planning it would spend a quarter losing to review sites. Recorded as a gap with the note that it becomes viable once the cluster has authority.

**Annotation.**
- Five pieces, one intent each, all linking to the pillar and to piece 3, which is the mechanism everything else depends on.
- Saying what was deliberately not planned, and why, is as useful as the plan. It stops the client asking for the head term every quarter.

## Example 2 — cannibalisation found

**Existing pages.** A 2024 blog post "Stripe reconciliation explained" and a 2025 product page section "Stripe reconciliation".

**Finding.** Both target the same informational intent; the product page outranks the post for some queries and the post for others, which splits links and confuses the intent signal.

**Recommendation.** Keep the blog post as the informational answer, cut the overlapping section from the product page to a single sentence and a link, and point both at the pillar. No new content needed for this term.

**Annotation.**
- The best SEO recommendation is often "write nothing". A junior adds a third page on the same subject and makes it worse.

## Example 3 — a brief that is usable

**Term.** "how to reconcile stripe payouts" (informational).
**Secondary.** "stripe payout report", "stripe fees reconciliation".

**Questions to answer** (from audience research and autocomplete): Why does the payout amount never match the invoices? Where do the fees appear? What about refunds that span two payouts? How do you handle a payout that crosses a month end? What do you give the auditor?

**Entities.** Balance transactions report, payout report, gross vs net, processing fees, refunds, chargebacks, bank feed, Xero, NetSuite, cut-off date, accruals.

**Angle.** Most existing results explain what a payout is. This one starts at the point where the numbers do not match, which is where the reader actually is.

**Not in this piece.** Adyen and GoCardless (their own pieces), software comparison (commercial piece).

**Title.** "How to reconcile Stripe payouts (and why they never match)" (57)

**Annotation.**
- The questions come from real phrasing, which is why the piece will answer what people asked rather than what the writer assumed.
- The entity list is what separates a knowledgeable piece from a summary. A writer who covers all eleven naturally has written the better article.
- "Not in this piece" prevents the cluster collapsing into one long article that ranks for nothing.

---

## Self-check applied
Every term has one intent; every brief has questions and entities; existing pages checked for overlap; titles within 60; nothing planned that the client cannot credibly win.
