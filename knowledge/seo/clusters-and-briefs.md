# Clusters, intent and content briefs

## Intent first

Every term gets one intent (informational, commercial, transactional, navigational) and one funnel stage. The intent decides the format: a "how to" term needs a guide, a "best X" term needs a comparison, a "X pricing" term needs a pricing page. Writing a blog post for a transactional term is the commonest wasted article in B2B.

## Clusters

A pillar covers the broad topic; supporting pieces cover the specific questions inside it and link up to the pillar and across to each other. The cluster is the unit of planning, not the individual post: one strong pillar with six supporting pieces beats seven unconnected articles on the same subject.

Before planning anything, read what the client already has (the Scout coverage). Two pages targeting the same intent compete with each other; that is cannibalisation, and the fix is usually to merge or to retarget one of them, not to write a third.

## Credibility

Only plan what the client can credibly win. A three-month-old domain does not rank for the category head term, and planning it wastes a quarter. Prefer specific, lower-volume terms where the client has genuine experience to show; experience is the part of E-E-A-T a company either has or does not, and it is visible in the writing.

## The brief

A brief that is a title and a word count produces a generic article. A usable brief contains:

- **Target term and its intent**, plus two or three secondary terms that belong to the same intent.
- **The questions the piece must answer**, phrased as the reader would ask them. Pull these from the audience research and from autocomplete, not from imagination.
- **The entities to cover**: the tools, standards, roles and concepts a knowledgeable piece would mention. Their absence is what makes thin content thin.
- **The angle**: what this piece says that the current results do not.
- **Internal links**: which existing pages to link to and from.
- **Title (≤ 60 chars) and meta description (≤ 155)**, written, not left to the writer.
- **What not to write**: the sub-topics that belong to a different piece in the cluster.

## On-page essentials

One H1 matching the intent, headings in a sensible order, the target term in the title and first hundred words without contortion, internal links with descriptive anchors, and a meta description that earns the click rather than repeating the title.
