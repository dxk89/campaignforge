# Copy checks

Every check the campaign passes run, over any copy you paste. It tells you
whether a line fits where you want to put it, whether it breaks the client's
own rules, and whether it reads like a machine wrote it.

It reports. It never rewrites, and it never saves what you check.

## Where to use it

**In the app.** Open **Check copy** in the top navigation. Paste the line,
pick a channel if you have one in mind, and pick the client if the copy is
for one of them. Picking the client is what turns a partial check into a
full one: without it, the client's avoid terms, approved claims and brand
spelling cannot be tested, and the result says so.

**From Claude.** The same checks run as an MCP tool, so you can ask Claude to
check a line while you are writing it, without opening anything. Setup is in
[`mcp/README.md`](../mcp/README.md).

## The rules

### Character limits

Every platform truncates, and each one truncates somewhere different. The
check reports where your line fits and where it does not, with how many
characters you are over.

Being too long only counts against you when you have named a channel. With no
channel chosen, a line that is too long for a Google headline may be exactly
right as a LinkedIn post, and marking it red would teach you to ignore red.

**What to do:** cut to the tightest limit you actually need, not the loosest
one available.

### avoid

**Trips on:** a word the client has asked not to use, from their voice rules.

**Why:** most clients have a handful of words they have decided against, often
for reasons that never got written down. Getting one into an ad is a small
thing that costs trust.

**What to do:** use the client's preferred term instead. The brand kit lists
them.

### competitor

**Trips on:** a competitor's name.

**Why:** naming a competitor in your own ad argues their case for them, and
puts their brand in front of your audience at your expense.

**What to do:** describe the alternative rather than naming it. "The
spreadsheet" beats naming the tool.

### superlative

**Trips on:** brochure words, the ones that sound like a claim without making
one.

**Why:** they make copy longer without making it more persuasive, and a reader
skips them.

**What to do:** replace the adjective with the fact underneath it. Not
"world-class accuracy", but the number.

### placeholder

**Trips on:** scaffolding left in the copy: square brackets, `lorem`, `TBD`,
`xx`.

**Why:** it ships. Somebody always assumes somebody else caught it.

**What to do:** fill it in or take it out.

### brand

**Trips on:** the brand name spelled or cased differently from the registered
form.

**Why:** the registered spelling is the one thing about a brand that is not a
matter of taste.

**What to do:** use the exact form in the brand kit.

### claim

**Trips on:** a number, a percentage, a currency figure or a comparison that
is not covered by an approved claim.

**Why:** this is the one rule here that is legal rather than stylistic. An
unapproved number in an ad is a liability, and it is the flag most worth
taking seriously.

**What to do:** get the claim approved, use one that already is, or take the
number out.

### pt-br

**Trips on:** Brazilian Portuguese forms in copy marked as European
Portuguese: "você", "gerenciar", "usuário".

**Why:** they read as foreign to a Portuguese audience in the way American
spelling reads to a British one.

**What to do:** use the European form. The glossary in the brand kit lists
the ones that matter.

### ai-word

**Trips on:** vocabulary that marks text as machine-written. Tier one, the
strongest tells, are violations: delve, leverage, robust, seamless,
comprehensive, holistic, elevate, unlock, transformative and their relatives.
Tier two and three are warnings, because they have honest uses.

**Why:** readers now recognise this register, and it makes copy read as
unedited output regardless of who wrote it.

**What to do:** say the plain thing. Most of these words can simply be
deleted without changing the meaning.

### ai-phrase

**Trips on:** a stock phrase: "in today's fast-paced world", "unlock the
potential of", "stands as a testament to", "at the forefront of".

**Why:** the same reason as the vocabulary, more so. These are the phrases
people quote when they are making fun of machine writing.

**What to do:** cut the phrase and keep the sentence.

### em-dash

**Trips on:** an em dash or an en dash.

**Why:** house style. Marketing copy generated by a model overuses them, and
avoiding them entirely is simpler than judging each one.

**What to do:** a comma, a semicolon, brackets, or two sentences.

### negative-parallelism

**Trips on:** "not just X, but Y" and its variants.

**Why:** a stock shape that reads as machine-written, and it usually hides a
weaker claim than saying Y on its own would.

**What to do:** say Y.

### copula-avoidance

**Trips on:** "serves as", "stands as", "acts as".

**Why:** it is a longer way of writing "is", and it sounds like it is
avoiding a commitment.

**What to do:** write "is".

### false-range

**Trips on:** "from X to Y" where X and Y are not two ends of a scale.

**Why:** it implies a spectrum that does not exist, so the reader looks for a
relationship between the two things and does not find one.

**What to do:** list them, or pick one.

### curly-quote

**Trips on:** a typographic quotation mark or apostrophe.

**Why:** most ad platform fields do not render them, so they arrive as
mojibake or get stripped.

**What to do:** use the straight version.

### decorative-emoji

**Trips on:** an emoji.

**Why:** in B2B copy it usually stands in for a point rather than making one,
and several platforms count it awkwardly against the character limit.

**What to do:** make the point.

## What it will not do

**It will not rewrite your copy.** Every check reports and leaves the decision
with you. This is deliberate and it is the same rule the campaign passes
follow: the tool flags, a person fixes. A checker that silently corrected
things would be a checker you could not trust to have shown you everything.

**It will not save anything you check.** Nothing pasted here is stored, on the
server or anywhere else. That is why there is no history: the trade is that
you cannot look back at what you checked last week, and in exchange there is
no question about where the copy went.

## For engineers

### The function

`web/core/check.js` exports `checkCopy(text, opts)`.

```js
const { checkCopy } = require('./web/core/check');

checkCopy('A robust platform.', {
  channel: 'linkedin',   // optional: report only this channel
  rules: buildRules(...) // optional: the client's rules
});
```

It returns:

```js
{
  text: string,
  chars: number,
  channel: string | null,
  fits:  [ { channel: 'linkedin', field: 'headline', limit: 70 } ],
  over:  [ { channel: 'google', field: 'headline', limit: 30, by: 12 } ],
  flags: [ { rule, detail, severity: 'violation' | 'warning', why } ],
  ranWithoutClientRules: boolean,
  verdict: 'clean' | 'warnings' | 'violations'
}
```

`verdict` exists so a caller acts on one field instead of counting
severities. `ranWithoutClientRules` exists because a clean result that
silently skipped half the rules is worse than no result.

### Where the checks live

| Check | Module |
|---|---|
| Character limits, ad and social | `web/core/limits.js` |
| avoid, competitor, superlative, placeholder, brand, claim, pt-br | `web/core/agents/tools/compliance.js` |
| The AI tells | `web/core/ai-tells.js`, called from `compliance.js` |

`checkCopy` calls `checkCompliance` once and lets it run the AI-tell
catalogue internally, rather than calling `findTells` separately. One path in
means the clinic and an agent pass cannot disagree about what is a violation.

### Adding a rule

Three edits, in this order:

1. Add the rule to the checker it belongs to, returning the usual
   `{ path, rule, detail, severity }`.
2. Add a sentence to `WHY` in `web/core/check.js`. Without one the flag falls
   back to pointing at this document, which helps nobody.
3. Add a case to `test/check.test.js`, and a case proving it stays quiet on
   copy that should pass. The second is the one that matters: a checker that
   fires on good copy gets switched off.

Then add its section to this document. `npm test` will not catch a missing
section, but Task 5 of the plan includes a check that every key in `WHY`
appears here, and it is worth running.

### Why `core/` is CommonJS

`web/core/` has no build step and is required by three things that do not
share a toolchain: the Next app through the `@core/*` alias, the retired
Express app in `legacy/`, and the MCP server in `mcp/`. Converting it to
TypeScript or ESM would break the last two.

Note that `@core/*` maps to the root `lib/` directory, not to `web/core/`.
A new module needs a one-line shim there:

```js
// lib/check.js
module.exports = require('../web/core/check');
```

Without it the module resolves in Node and fails only in the Next build.
